Sample session

Introduction to generalized linear mixed models (Module B3, session 1)

Open the slides Do the exercises Take the self-check

Context

  • Audience: early-career PhD researchers who have used linear models but have not met mixed models before.
  • Place in the curriculum: first session of Module B3, Mixed models. It builds on B1 (linear models) and B2 (GLMs).
  • Length: a 15-minute version for the demonstration; in the course, the same input is followed by about an hour of hands-on work.

Learning goals

By the end of the session, students will be able to:

  1. Recognise when their data are grouped (repeated trials, siblings, individuals in social groups) and why that breaks the independence assumption.
  2. Explain what a random intercept is and what the intraclass correlation tells them.
  3. Choose between a linear mixed model and a GLMM based on the type of their response.
  4. Interpret the coefficients of a GLMM as effects for the typical individual.

In the full course session

After the input, students:

  1. Sketch the hierarchy of their own dataset (5 minutes, then compare with a neighbour).
  2. Work through the exercises: fit a first random-intercept model to the lizard data in the browser, then adapt the code to their own data.
  3. Compute and interpret the ICC, and discuss: does the grouping matter for their question?
  4. Take the self-check to see which ideas need another look.
